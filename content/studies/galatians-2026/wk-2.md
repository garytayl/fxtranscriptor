## Study Questions

*"Gospel" in Greek is εὐαγγέλιον (transliterated as euangelion), which translates literally to "good news" or "glad tidings". Derived from eu ("good") and angelia ("message" or "tidings"), it was used in the New Testament to describe the announcement of salvation through Jesus Christ* (Yahweh is Salvation the Messiah)*.* The word gospel is used 76 times in the New Testament. Galatians uses the word gospel more than any other new testament book.

### **READ: [Galatians 2](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Gal.2&options=HNVUG)**

1. According to [Galatians 1:6,](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Gal.1.6&options=HNVUG) what is Paul's major concern in his letter to the Galatian churches?
    1. How is this still an issue we have to contend with today?
2. After 14 years of preaching and planting churches to the Gentiles, why does Paul go back to Jerusalem? (Gal 2:1-5)
    1. Who does He go with? (Gal 2:1)
    2. What do we know about Barnabas and Titus? see: [**Acts 4:36, 9:27, 11:22-24, 13:2, 2 Corinthians 8:6, 16, 23, 12:18, Titus 1:4-5**](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Acts.4.36%20Acts.9.27%20Acts.11.22-Acts.11.24%20Acts.13.2%202Cor.8.6%202Cor.8.16%202Cor.8.23%202Cor.12.18%20Titus.1.4-Titus.1.5&options=HNVUG)
3. Many people were very interested to go to Jerusalem in Paul's day just as people are in our day. Why was Paul seemingly not interested (14 years) and seemingly even bothered that he had to go back?
    1. How might this be a good lesson for us?
4. Why was issue such an important issue that it had to pull Paul, Barnabas, and Titus off the mission of preaching, discipling, and serving?
    1. What are some of the issues that we may have to contend for the truth of the gospel in our day that may necessarily pull us off our primary mission of preaching, discipling, and serving the lost?

**READ: [Deuteronomy 19:15, Matthew 7:3-5, Matthew 18:15-17, Galatians 6:1, 1 Timothy 5:19-21](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Deut.19.15%20Matt.7.3-Matt.7.5%20Matt.18.15-Matt.18.17%20Gal.6.1%201Tim.5.19-1Tim.5.21&options=HNVUG)**

1. How was Paul's approach to confronting the controversy of circumcision and the Old Testament Mosaic law for Gentiles in Galatians 2 in obedience to the Scriptures we just read?
    1. How can these verses and Paul's example help us as we contend for the truth of the gospel?
2. God had Paul write, *"to watch out for those who cause divisions and create obstacles contrary to the doctrine that you have been taught; avoid them."* ([Romans 16:17](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Rom.16.17&options=VNHUG)) God also had Paul write that the church should turn men "*over to Satan"* if it is found that they will not repent of sin and not believe the truth of the gospel in the church. ([1 Corinthians 5:5, 1 Timothy 1:20](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=1Cor.5.5%201Tim.1.20&options=HNVUG))
Why is all of that important to remember as we fight in grace for the truth of the gospel in relationships like Paul modeled?
3. For the sake of the truth of the gospel, why didn't Paul just go along to get along with those calling themselves Christians when they showed up in the Galatian churches? ([Gal 2:3-5](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Gal.2.3-Gal.2.5&options=VNHUG))

[**Acts 15**](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Acts.15&options=VNHUG) gives us the details of how the meeting in Jerusalem played out.

1. What was the final decision of the church apostles? ([Gal 2:6-10)](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Gal.2.6-Gal.2.10&options=VNHUG)
2. In Acts 15:15-21, James settles the dispute with all the opinions, experiences, and testimonies of all those involved by quoting the scriptures. How is this an important model for us as we fight for the truth of the gospel?
3. What are "religious traditions", that may seem biblical, that leaders say people must do to prove they and their family really believe in Jesus?
    1. Why throughout history have men who called themselves believers come up with those types of ways adding to the gospel?

**READ: [Acts 16:1-4, 1 Corinthians 9:19-23](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Acts.16.1-Acts.16.4%201Cor.9.19-1Cor.9.23&options=HNVUG)**

1. Titus, who was fully greek, was not *"compelled to be circumcised"* (Gal 2:3).  Paul, who fought against the necessity of circumcision, made Timothy, who was half Israelite, be circumcised before he could go with Paul on his missionary journey's. Why according to these Scriptures?
    1. How is this a great lesson for us as we seek to be equipped to serve the Lord?
2. In Galatians 2:4 Paul writes, "*false brothers…came in secretly to spy on the freedom that we have in Christ Jesus, in order to enslave us."* In 1 Corinthians 9:19-23 Paul writes, *"I have made myself a slave to everyone, in order to win more people."*
    1. Discuss what Paul means in using slavery in these examples?
    2. How can people often deviate from the truth if the gospel *"in order to win more people"* including being about only certain people?
    3. How can we be sure that we are not deviating from the truth of the gospel "*in order to win more people"*?

**READ: [Mark 9:33-35, Romans 12:3, Philippians 2:3-4, 2 Corinthians 12:9-10](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Mark.9.33-Mark.9.35%20Rom.12.3%202Cor.12.9-2Cor.12.10%20Phil.2.3-Phil.2.4&options=HNVUG)**

1. What is often at the heart of people deviating from the truth of the gospel like *Cephas* (Peter), *men who came from James*, and *Barnabas* forcing men like Paul to have to confront publicly? ([Gal 2:11-21](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version=HCSB@reference=Gal.2.11-Gal.2.21&options=VNHUG))
    1. Why do pastoral leaders not confront other pastoral leaders like this in our day? (private/public face to face)
2. What does Paul say the *"hypocrisy"* is?
    1. How is this very typical of humanity even believers?
3. What does it mean that believers *"know that no one is justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ",* and "*if righteousness comes through the law, then Christ died for nothing."?*
4. Paul says, *"I have died to the law, so that I might live for God."*
How does asking the question of how we *"might live for Christ"* of utmost importance before we start looking for the rules and laws God wants us to live by?
5. Jesus said, *"pick up your cross and follow me."* Paul says, *"I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me.* What does this look like to live this out mentally and practically?
6. How are you tempted to "*set aside the grace of God"* to try and make things right with God and people? (Gal 2:21)
7. Paul writes, *"is Christ then a promoter of sin? Absolutely not!".* Jesus said, *"Don't assume that I came to destroy the Law or the Prophets. I did not come to destroy but to fulfill."* (Matt 5:17)
What are they both saying?
8. In 70 AD the after the destruction of the temple, the Jerusalem church is scattered and ceases to exit as it was established and practiced. The gentile churches spread to all the known world.
Why was this important, and what can it teach us about the truth of the gospel message, the church, and taking the gospel to the world?

### **FOLLOW UP**
