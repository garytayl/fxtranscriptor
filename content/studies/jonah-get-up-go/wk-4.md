# Wk 4: Is it right to be angry?

Instead, Jonah runs from God. When confronted, Jonah would rather die than obey God. In his darkest moment, he turns to God. Given another chance, Jonah obeys, but with a bad attitude. Join us as we explore Jonah and examine our **Get Up! Go!** *#Jonah #2026*

## Study Questions

### RECAP

*"The word of the Lord came to Jonah son of Amittai: "Get up! Go to the great city of Nineveh and preach against it, because their wickedness has confronted Me." However, Jonah got up to flee…from the Lord's presence. Then the Lord hurled a violent wind on the sea and appointed a huge fish to swallow Jonah, and Jonah was in the fish three days and three nights. Jonah prayed to the Lord his God from inside the fish." God has the fish spit Jonah out. Jonah* chooses to *"Get up! Go to the great city of Nineveh and preach the message." "The men of Nineveh believed in God."! A public repentance was called for and practiced. "God saw their actions—that they had turned from their evil ways—so God relented from the disaster."*

### READ: [Jonah 4:1-11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=%20Jonah%204%3A1-11&version=HCSB)

1. Why was Jonah angry?

2. 2 Kings 14:25 tells us that Jonah was known as the prophet that prophesied the success and reestablishment of the northern border of Israel. Today, he is barely known for that prophesy and the decades of peace and prosperity that followed.

   In light of what was prophesied by the other prophets about Assyria and what Assyria would do to Israel (in just 40 years), how does that make it easier to understand Jonah's anger?

3. What does Jonah immediately do in the midst of his anger?

4. James 4:3 explains Jonah's prayer, *"You ask and don't receive because you ask with wrong motives, so that you may spend it on your evil desires."* How do believers today in the midst of their displeasure and anger pray prayers like Jonah?
   - What do our prayers expose about our hearts?

**READ: [Exodus 34:5-7, Psalm 103:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2034%3A5-7%2C%20Psalm%20103%3A8&version=HCSB)**

1. Jonah prays, *"isn't this what I said while I was still in my own country?…I knew that You are a merciful and compassionate God, slow to become angry, rich in faithful love, and One who relents from sending disaster."* We are given insight into the fact that Jonah actually believed God's character from the beginning of God asking him to go to Nineveh. He simply tried to rejected and refuse the part of God's character he did not want to share with others.
   - How do we see people (even those who claim to be believers) refuse to share God's full character in our day?
   - What tends to happen to people emotionally when they reject part of God's character?

2. Many today can act like they are much more spiritual than Jonah saying that they can't understand why Jonah would be so upset. Be honest. How do you get angry in the same ways as Jonah?

As you read these verses, note what God say about anger.

**READ: [Proverbs 10:12, 14:17, 15:1, 18, 16:32, 19:11, 20:3, 22:24-25, 29:9, 11-12, 37:8-9, Psalm 4:4, 37:8, Ecclesiastes 7:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs%2010%3A12%2C%2014%3A17%2C%2015%3A1%2C%2018%2C%2016%3A32%2C%2019%3A11%2C%2020%3A3%2C%2022%3A24-25%2C%2029%3A9%2C%2011-12%2C%2037%3A8-9%2C%20Psalm%204%3A4%2C%2037%3A8%2C%20Ecclesiastes%207%3A9%20&version=HCSB)**

1. Anger is an emotion that comes from God. What does God say about anger in these verses?

2. Psychologists often say that depression and anger are two sides of the same coin. How do we see this in the life of Jonah?

3. Jonah has a death wish as a result of things not going the way he wanted. How is this a normal response in Scripture (think of other biblical characters) and humanity?
   - Have you ever thought about how anger over earthly life circumstances leads us to a desire for death to ourselves and others?

As you read these verses, note what God say about how to handle anger in the body of believers the church.

**READ: [Romans 12:18-21, 1 Corinthians 13:4-5, Galatians 5:19-21, Ephesians 4:26, 31, Philippians 4:4-8, Colossians 3:8, 1 Timothy 2:8, Titus 1:7-10, 1 Thessalonians 5:16-24, James 1:19-23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2012%3A18-21%2C%201%20Corinthians%2013%3A4-5%2C%20Galatians%205%3A19-21%2C%20Ephesians%204%3A26%2C%2031%2C%20Philippians%204%3A4-8%2C%20Colossians%203%3A8%2C%201%20Timothy%202%3A8%2C%20Titus%201%3A7-10%2C%201%20Thessalonians%205%3A16-24%2C%20James%201%3A19-23&version=HCSB)**

1. What do these verses say about how to handle anger in the body of believers the church?
   - Why don't we do these things and instead act like Jonah?
   - Why do you think most of the letters in the new testament speak to the churches about the issue of anger?

**READ: [Matthew 23:1-3, 11-15, 29-30, 37](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2023%3A1-3%2C%2011-15%2C%2029-30%2C%2037&version=HCSB)**

1. The religious leaders of Jesus day tended to be very pious, serious, and angry like Jonah. How do we see people have these same tendencies in our day?
   - Discuss how the remedies Jesus offers in this passage are still the same for us?

2. How does Jonah respond to the Ninevites repentance, and God's relenting? Why? (Jonah 4:5)

3. How is the alone pity party like Jonah often the response of those stuck in anger?

4. How does God respond to Jonah's hard heart? (Jonah 4:6)
   - How does this again show Jonah the part of God's character he does not want to embrace?

5. God asks, *"Is it right for you to be angry about the plant?"* Fill in the blank with you and the Lord, Is it right for you to be angry about ___________? What are the plants and worms in your life that you keep having the right to be angry about but are distracting you from worshipping God and serving others?

**READ: [Job 1:20-22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Job%201%3A20-22&version=HCSB)**

1. What was Job's response to losing everything except his wife? (wealth, health, children, relationships, influence)
   - How is Job's response the proper response in our lives vs. Jonah's response?

2. The book finishes with God saying, *"Should I not care about the great city of Nineveh, which has more than 120,000 people who cannot distinguish between their right and their left, as well as many animals?"* As far as we know, the book ends with Jonah still holding on to his anger.

   Is there any anger that you are holding onto that God wants you to let go of embracing the truth of his character so that a root of bitterness ([Hebrews 12:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2012%3A15&version=HCSB)) does not take hold in your life like it did Jonah?

**READ: [Luke 11:29-33](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2011%3A29-33&version=HCSB)**

1. What sign was Jonah looking for as he watched Nineveh?
   - What sign did God give him on the 40th day?
   - How can we in our anger and sadness be looking for all the wrong signs?

2. In Luke 15:7 Jesus says, *"there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over 99 righteous people who don't need repentance."* If we are going to experience the joy of heaven what will we need to be focused on doing and celebrating?
   - What might be our excuses for not rejoicing in true repentance when we see it like Jonah saw it?

3. How do we know the repentance of the people of Nineveh was spiritually genuine?

### FOLLOW UP

- Do you believe that the Lord is compassionate and gracious God, slow to anger and rich in faithful love and truth and that Jesus was the embodiment of it? Will you surrender your life to Him?
- Is it right for you to be angry about ___________?
- What will you do with the reality of your anger according to Scriptures we looked at today?
- What are some key takeaways that God wants you to wrestle with and pursue as a result of the truths of Jonah?

Also, remember that it is ok to say I don't know, but I will try to find out.*

## re:group Reminder

*At fxchurch, we desire to be a humble and confessional people by modeling it ourselves first.
